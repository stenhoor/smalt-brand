[contributors](/community/get-involved#join-the-core-team).

### Steve TenHoor

**Organizing founder** · [@stenhoor on GitHub](https://github.com/stenhoor)

Steve TenHoor has been in technology for more than three decades, long enough to have met data in every size, from
Oracle and SQL Server down to Access, CSV files and the occasional heroic Excel sheet. He has built software, led
development teams, and never quite gotten over his crush on Python, Jupyter, marimo, pandas and Polars. smaltworks is
what happens when that turns into a question: why does exploring data need so much setup? His answer is small, fast
and in the browser, on the machine you already have. Stay smalty.

### Andrew Beers

**Core team** · [@apbeers on GitHub](https://github.com/apbeers)

Andrew Beers has spent 13 years in IT, software development, and cybersecurity, building everything from apps to the systems that keep them safe. He has a soft spot for software that does exactly what it needs to—and no more. That instinct helped shape smalt: build the functionality ourselves instead of leaning on third-party libraries, and turn reusable UI into Kits built for the job without the extra bloat. For Andrew, the right abstractions make powerful tools feel simple and leave room for what comes next.

### Jake

**Chief Smalty Officer (self-appointed)** · [@beepboopbop64 on GitHub](https://github.com/beepboopbop64)

Jake works with data for a living, which mostly means writing a query, pressing run, and then waiting. And waiting.
Enough hours staring at another Spark pool spinning up will give anyone strong opinions about loading spinners, and Jake
has rated every one of them. smaltworks is the antidote: open a tab, load the data, get an answer while the coffee is
still hot. Jake signed on for the speed, stayed for the pigment, and is treating the whole thing as a fun adventure:
rose-tinted shades on, data in the browser, and every hot take served with a grain of smalt.

> "I've waited on so many Spark pools I could tell you how each one feels about Mondays. Life's too short, and my
> laptop is already on. Pass the smalt."

Stay smalty.
